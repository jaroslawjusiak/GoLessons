# Go for Beginners — One-Year Course Syllabus

**Target audience:** total beginners (≈30 kyu) to intermediate (≈10 kyu)
**Format:** 30 weekly meetings, ~4 hours each; every meeting opens with a 10–20 minute lecture
**Board progression:** 9×9 (rules & basics) → 13×13 (strategy transition) → 19×19 (full-board play)

---

## Course Overview

| Phase | Lectures | Board | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|-------|
| 1. Foundations | 1–8 | 9×9 | Rules, territory, life & death, capture |
| 2. First Strategy | 9–15 | 9×9 → 13×13 | Shapes, strength, sente, attack & defence |
| 3. The Full Board | 16–30 | 19×19 | Opening, joseki, direction, invasions, aji, yose |

Each lecture ends with a short "home study" list: a tsumego (life-and-death puzzle) set and reading/video references. Participants should play at least one or two 9×9 (or larger) games between meetings.

## Phase 1 — Foundations on the 9×9 Board

### Lecture 1 — What Is Go?
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Understand what Go is, its object, and the culture of the game.
- A 4,000-year-old game — brief history and spread to the modern world
- Equipment: board, stones (black & white), bowls; board sizes 9×9, 13×13, 19×19
- The object: control more territory than the opponent
- Placing stones on intersections (not inside squares)
- Black plays first; alternating turns
- Etiquette and respect at the board
- First game experience: play freely on 9×9
- **References**
  - Book: *Go: A Complete Introduction to the Game* — Cho Chikun (intro chapters)
  - Book: *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 1* — Janice Kim & Jeong Soo-hyun
  - Video: search "what is go baduk weiqi" (Go Magic channel); "Go for beginners" intro videos
  - Online: https://online-go.com (sign up and play your first game)

### Lecture 2 — Liberties, Capture, and Atari
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Master the basic mechanics of capture.
- What is a liberty (the empty points touching a stone/group)
- Capturing: removing a stone when its last liberty is filled
- **Atari** — a stone or group with only one liberty left
- Connecting stones into groups
- Running away from atari; extending to gain liberties
- Self-capture is illegal (no suicide)
- Practice: capture races on a tiny board
- **References**
  - Book: *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 1* (capture chapters)
  - Video: search "go atari capture liberties beginners" (Go Magic / NYIG)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Atari

### Lecture 3 — What Is Territory?
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Understand territory and how the game is scored.
- Territory = surrounded empty points controlled by your stones
- How surrounding works: walls, borders
- Counting territory (Japanese scoring) — points of empty area plus captures
- Komi (compensation for White playing second) — mention, detail later
- The relationship between stones and territory
- A quick sample 9×9 game scored move by move
- **References**
  - Book: *The Second Book of Go* — Richard Bozulich (territory & counting)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Territory
  - Online: https://www.usgo.org (rules and scoring pages)

### Lecture 4 — Life and Death: Eyes
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Learn how groups live and how to kill them.
- A group needs two eyes to be unconditionally alive
- What an "eye" is (a single surrounded internal point)
- Why one eye is not enough — filling the last liberty
- Two eyes = immortal group
- One-eyed groups can be attacked and killed
- Alive vs. dead shapes on 9×9
- **References**
  - Book: *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 2* — Janice Kim (eyes & life)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Eye
  - Video: search "go two eyes life and death beginner"

### Lecture 5 — Two Eyes, False Eyes, and Nakade
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Distinguish real eyes from false eyes; kill groups from inside.
- Real eyes vs. **false eyes** (where stones can be captured)
- Why false eyes do not guarantee life
- **Nakade** — killing a shape from the inside
- Classic killing shapes: straight three, bent three, bulky five, rabbitty six
- The rule: "four is alive, five in a row is dead, etc." (key tsumego shapes)
- Practice: life-and-death problems (tsumego)
- **References**
  - Book: *Life and Death* — James Davies (Elementary Go Series)
  - Book: *Graded Go Problems for Beginners, Vol. 1–2* — Kano Yoshinori
  - Online: https://tsumego-hero.com (free daily tsumego)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Nakade

### Lecture 6 — The Ko Rule
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Understand ko and why the repetition rule exists.
- What is **ko** (the shape allowing immediate recapture)
- The ko rule: you must play elsewhere before recapturing
- **Ko threats** — moves that force the opponent to answer
- Simple ko fights and how to win them
- Why the rule prevents infinite repetition
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Ko
  - Book: *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 2* (ko chapter)
  - Video: search "go ko rule ko threat"

### Lecture 7 — Ending the Game, Counting, and Scoring
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Properly finish and score a game.
- When the game ends (both players pass)
- Confirming dead stones and removing them
- Filling dame (neutral points)
- Counting territory + prisoners
- Komi explained in full
- A complete scored game from start to finish
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Scoring
  - Online: https://www.britgo.org (rules & scoring)
  - Book: *Go: A Complete Introduction to the Game* (scoring chapter)

### Lecture 8 — Basic Capture Techniques
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Learn the fundamental capturing patterns.
- **Ladder (shicho)** — chasing a stone in a zigzag; when it works/fails
- **Net (geta)** — capturing with a surrounding move
- **Snapback** — sacrificing to capture
- The dangers of a broken ladder (ladder breaker)
- Practice: capture problems
- **References**
  - Book: *Tesuji* — James Davies (ladder & net)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Ladder
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/NetGo

## Phase 2 — First Strategy (9×9 → 13×13)

### Lecture 9 — Corners, Sides, and the Centre
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Understand why corners are worth more than sides and the centre.
- Corners are the most efficient place to make territory
- Sides are next; the centre is the least efficient
- "Corner, side, then centre" as the first strategic principle
- Making territory with fewer stones
- How this shapes early play on 9×9
- **References**
  - Book: *In the Beginning* — Ishikura Noboru (Elementary Go Series)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/CornersSidesCenter

### Lecture 10 — Good Shape and Bad Shape
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Introduce the concept of shape and efficiency.
- What is "shape" — how stones work together
- The **empty triangle** — the classic bad (inefficient) shape
- Good shapes: **bamboo joint**, **table shape**, **tiger's mouth**
- Efficiency: doing more with fewer stones
- Shape as the foundation of good play
- **References**
  - Book: *Making Good Shape* — Richard Bozulich & Rob van Zeijst
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Shape
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/EmptyTriangle
  - Video: search "good shape bad shape go empty triangle"

### Lecture 11 — Connections and Cuts
**Board:** 9×9
**Goal:** Understand connecting your own stones and cutting your opponent's.
- Why groups must stay connected
- The **cut** — separating the opponent's stones; "cut is stronger than connect"
- Solid connection vs. diagonal connection vs. hanging connection
- Cutting points and defending them
- The power of separated weak groups
- **References**
  - Book: *The Second Book of Go* — Richard Bozulich (cut & connect)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Cut
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Connection

### Lecture 12 — Strong Groups and Weak Groups
**Board:** 9×9 → 13×13
**Goal:** Evaluate the strength of groups and understand bases.
- What makes a group strong (eyes, base, room to live)
- What makes a group weak (no base, few liberties, no eye space)
- The **base** of a group
- Attacking does not always mean killing — it means gaining profit
- Defending weak groups before it is too late
- **References**
  - Book: *Attack and Defense* — Ishida Akira & James Davies (Elementary Go Series)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Base

### Lecture 13 — Sente and Gote
**Board:** 13×13
**Goal:** Understand the tempo concepts that govern all Go decisions.
- **Sente** — a move that must be answered; keeping the initiative
- **Gote** — a move that ends the exchange, giving initiative away
- Taking sente and giving it up
- Why sente moves are so valuable
- Identifying sente in a real position
- **References**
  - Book: *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* — Kageyama Toshiro
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Sente
  - Video: search "what is sente gote go"

### Lecture 14 — How to Attack and How to Defend
**Board:** 13×13
**Goal:** Learn the basic ideas of attacking and defending groups.
- Attacking for profit, not for a kill
- Surrounding vs. touching (don't touch a weak group you attack)
- Defending by making eyes and extending
- The value of thickness created while attacking
- Choosing between attack and defence
- **References**
  - Book: *Attack and Defense* — Ishida Akira & James Davies
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Attack
  - Video: search "go how to attack weak groups"

### Lecture 15 — Transition to the 13×13 Board
**Board:** 13×13
**Goal:** Move from small-board fighting to full-board thinking.
- Why 13×13 is the bridge board
- Opening on 13×13: corners, sides, centre
- **Extensions** — how far to extend from a corner stone
- Whole-board awareness: not just local fighting
- A guided 13×13 game review
- **References**
  - Book: *Opening Theory Made Easy* — Otake Hideo
  - Online: https://online-go.com (play 13×13 games)
  - Video: search "go opening 13x13 beginners"

## Phase 3 — The Full 19×19 Board

### Lecture 16 — The 19×19 Board and Opening Theory
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Understand the scale of the full board and basic opening strategy.
- The size of the board and the value of each region
- First moves: occupy the empty corners
- The opening sequence: corners → enclosures/extensions → sides → centre
- Star points (hoshi) and why they mark the board
- A complete 19×19 opening demonstration
- **References**
  - Book: *In the Beginning* — Ishikura Noboru
  - Book: *Opening Theory Made Easy* — Otake Hideo
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/OpeningTheory

### Lecture 17 — The Corner Points: 3-3, 3-4, 4-4, 5-4
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Know the main corner openings and what they mean.
- **3-4 (komoku)** — balanced between territory and influence
- **4-4 (hoshi)** — influence and speed, open to the 3-3 invasion
- **3-3 (san-san)** — secure corner territory, low influence
- **5-4 (takamoku)** — high influence
- Choosing a corner point to match your style
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/CornerOpenings
  - Book: *In the Beginning* (corner points chapter)
  - Video: search "go corner opening 3-4 4-4 3-3"

### Lecture 18 — Enclosures, Extensions, and Pincers
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn how to develop the corners and sides.
- **Enclosures (shimari)** — securing a corner with two stones
- The one-space, small-knight, and large-knight enclosures
- **Extensions** — two-space and three-space extensions along the side
- The **pincer (hasami)** — preventing the opponent's extension
- Balancing enclosure, extension, and pincer
- **References**
  - Book: *38 Basic Joseki* — Kosugi Kiyoshi & James Davies
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Enclosure
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Extension

### Lecture 19 — Simple Joseki
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn a few standard corner sequences and why they are "even".
- What is a **joseki** (an established, even corner sequence)
- Approach moves (kakari) to the corners
- Basic joseki for the 3-4 point (low and high approach)
- Basic joseki for the 4-4 point
- Why joseki are fair — and when to deviate
- **References**
  - Book: *38 Basic Joseki* — Kosugi Kiyoshi & James Davies
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Joseki
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Kakari

### Lecture 20 — The Direction of Play
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn to play moves that work with the whole board.
- Playing toward the larger, more open side
- Pushing your opponent toward your own strength
- Away from thickness, toward weakness
- How direction determines the value of a move
- Seeing the whole board before playing locally
- **References**
  - Book: *The Direction of Play* — Kajiwara Takeo
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/DirectionOfPlay
  - Video: search "go direction of play"

### Lecture 21 — Influence vs. Territory: Keeping the Balance
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Understand the two kinds of value and how to balance them.
- **Territory** — secured points now
- **Influence (thickness)** — power that creates points later
- Converting influence into territory or an attack
- Over-investing in one or the other
- Keeping the balance in your own games
- **References**
  - Book: *In the Beginning* (influence chapters)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Influence
  - Video: search "go influence vs territory thickness"

### Lecture 22 — The Efficiency of Moves
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Maximise the value of every stone.
- Doing the most work with the fewest stones
- **Overconcentration** — too many stones for too little
- Slow (small) moves vs. efficient (big) moves
- Reviewing your moves for efficiency
- How efficiency wins games
- **References**
  - Book: *Basic Techniques of Go* — Haruyama Isamu & Nagahara Yoshiaki
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Efficiency

### Lecture 23 — Proverbs I: Urgent Moves Before Big Moves
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn to prioritise using classical proverbs.
- **"Urgent before big"** — safety before profit
- **"Play away from thickness"**
- **"Don't touch a weak group"** (when attacking)
- **"The opponent's vital point is my vital point"**
- Applying proverbs as guidelines, not laws
- **References**
  - Book: *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* — Kageyama Toshiro
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/GoProverbs

### Lecture 24 — Proverbs II: Hane at the Head of Two (or Three) Stones
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn the key fighting proverbs.
- **"Hane at the head of two stones"** and of three stones
- **"Don't peep where you can cut"**
- **"Cut the side you don't want"**
- **"Capture the cutting stone"**
- Using proverbs in real fights
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/HaneAtTheHeadOfTwoStones
  - Book: *Tesuji* — James Davies
  - Video: search "go proverbs hane at the head"

### Lecture 25 — Invasions
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn to enter and live inside the opponent's potential territory.
- What is an **invasion** (entering a large framework)
- Living inside with a small group
- The 3-3 invasion of the 4-4 point
- Choosing when to invade vs. reduce
- Surviving an invasion (making eyes in a small space)
- **References**
  - Book: *Attack and Defense* — Ishida Akira & James Davies (invasion chapter)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Invasion
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/ThreeThreeInvasion

### Lecture 26 — Reductions (Keshi)
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn to reduce large frameworks from the outside.
- **Reduction (keshi)** — shrinking a framework from outside
- Invasion vs. reduction: when to choose which
- The **cap** and the **shoulder hit** as reducing moves
- Playing lightly while reducing
- Managing the risk of a reduction
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Reduction
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/ShoulderHit
  - Book: *Attack and Defense* (reduction examples)

### Lecture 27 — Using Influence (Thickness)
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Convert influence and thickness into profit.
- What thickness is really for — attacking, not building walls of territory
- Attacking with a thick wall
- The danger of using thickness to make territory too close
- Inviting the opponent to approach your thickness
- Turning influence into a large framework
- **References**
  - Book: *Attack and Defense* — Ishida Akira & James Davies
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Thickness
  - Video: search "how to use thickness go influence"

### Lecture 28 — Aji
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Understand latent possibilities (aji) and how to use them.
- What is **aji** — the lingering potential in a position
- Good aji vs. bad aji
- Using aji to make moves later (e.g., a peep or a cut)
- Removing bad aji to avoid future trouble
- Keeping aji as a threat instead of playing it immediately
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Aji
  - Book: *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* — Kageyama Toshiro
  - Video: search "go aji explained"

### Lecture 29 — Sabaki
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Learn to handle your own weak stones lightly and flexibly.
- What is **sabaki** — settling a weak group lightly
- Sacrificing stones and forcing moves
- Making shape, not just running
- Light play vs. heavy play
- Escaping while making the opponent overconcentrated
- **References**
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Sabaki
  - Book: *Basic Techniques of Go* — Haruyama Isamu & Nagahara Yoshiaki
  - Video: search "go sabaki light play"

### Lecture 30 — Yose: The Endgame
**Board:** 19×19
**Goal:** Play the endgame correctly and understand counting.
- What is the **yose** (endgame) — when the game is decided in small moves
- **Sente endgame**, **gote endgame**, and **reverse sente**
- Counting the value of endgame moves
- The order of endgame play
- Turning a close game into a win in the endgame
- Course wrap-up and a roadmap to sdk (single-digit kyu) and beyond
- **References**
  - Book: *The Endgame* — Tomoko Ogawa & James Davies (Elementary Go Series)
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Yose
  - Online: https://senseis.xmp.net/Endgame
  - Video: search "go endgame yose counting"
