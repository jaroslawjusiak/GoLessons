# Go Course Syllabus: From 30k to 10k
**Target Audience:** Beginners to Intermediate Players (30k - 10k)
**Duration:** 1 Year (30 Meetings)
**Meeting Structure:** 10-20 minute focused lecture, followed by 3.5 hours of guided play, Tsumego (life & death) solving, and game reviews.

---

## Phase 1: The Foundations (9x9 Board)
*Goal: Understand the rules, the ultimate goal of the game, basic capturing, and fundamental life and death. All play and examples are restricted to the 9x9 board.*

### Lecture 1: The Rules and the Goal of Go
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Placing stones on intersections.
  * Liberties and capturing stones.
  * The rule of Ko.
  * The ultimate goal: Surrounding territory (not just capturing).
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "How to Play Go" by Go Magic (YouTube).
  * *Reading:* Sensei's Library - "Rules of Go".

### Lecture 2: Life and Death Basics (The Two Eyes)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is an "eye"?
  * Why one eye is not enough (the concept of false eyes).
  * The rule of two eyes: making a group immortal.
  * Basic 9x9 life and death shapes (straight four, bent four, bulky five).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Life and Death" by James Davies (Chapters 1-2).
  * *Website:* Go Magic - "Life and Death" beginner module.

### Lecture 3: Connecting and Cutting
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Why we connect (reducing liberties, preventing capture).
  * How to cut (separating enemy stones to attack them).
  * Solid connections vs. diagonal connections vs. jump connections.
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "Connecting and Cutting" by Nicktoof (YouTube).
  * *Practice:* 9x9 Tsumego focusing purely on cutting and connecting.

### Lecture 4: Sente and Gote (The Concept of Initiative)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Definitions: Sente (initiative/forcing) vs. Gote (responding).
  * Why playing in sente is crucial.
  * How to create sente (threats to capture or kill).
  * The danger of playing in gote unnecessarily.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go" by Toshiro Kageyama (Chapter on Sente/Gote).
  * *Article:* "Sente and Gote" on Sensei's Library.

### Lecture 5: Territory vs. Influence on 9x9
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Defining territory (secure, counted points).
  * Defining influence (potential, outward-facing power).
  * How to count territory at the end of a 9x9 game.
  * Transitioning from influence to territory.
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "Territory vs Influence" by Dwyrin (YouTube - Back to Basics series).
  * *Reading:* "In the Beginning" by Ishida Yoshio (Chapter 1).

### Lecture 6: Basic 9x9 Strategy and Openings
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Where to play the first move on 9x9 (center vs. corners).
  * The concept of the "base" (making a group alive quickly).
  * Expanding your own territory vs. reducing the opponent's.
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "9x9 Go Strategy" by Go Magic (YouTube).
  * *Practice:* Play 9x9 games focusing only on securing a base.

### Lecture 7: Introduction to Tsumego (Life & Death Puzzles)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * How to study Tsumego effectively (don't guess, read it out).
  * Basic attacking techniques: placement, throw-ins.
  * Basic defending techniques: expanding space, making eyes.
* **References:** 
  * *App/Website:* Go Problems app, or Tsumego on OGS (Online Go Server).
  * *Book:* "500 1-dan Tsumego" (for later), but start with "1000 Life and Death Problems" (Beginner section).

### Lecture 8: 9x9 Endgame (Yose) Basics
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is the endgame?
  * Identifying the "biggest" moves.
  * Sente endgame moves vs. Gote endgame moves.
  * Basic counting (1 point, 2 points, 3 point moves).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Endgame" by James Davies (Chapter 1).
  * *Video:* "Basic Endgame" by Nicktoof (YouTube).

## Phase 2: Strategy and Shapes (13x13 Board)
*Goal: Transition to a larger board. Introduce spatial concepts, good/bad shapes, and the fundamentals of attacking and defending. Play moves to the 13x13 board.*

### Lecture 9: Transition to 13x13 and the Golden Rule
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * How the 13x13 board changes the game.
  * The Golden Rule: Corners first, then sides, then the center.
  * Why corners are the most efficient (two walls already provided by the board).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "In the Beginning" by Ishida Yoshio (Chapters 1-3).
  * *Video:* "The Golden Rule of Go" by Dwyrin (YouTube).

### Lecture 10: Good Shape vs. Bad Shape
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is "shape" and why efficiency matters.
  * Bad shapes: Empty triangle, dumpling shape, heavy shapes.
  * Good shapes: Bamboo joint, ponnuki, tiger's mouth, keima (knight's move).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go" by Toshiro Kageyama (Chapter on Shape).
  * *Article:* "Shape" on Sensei's Library.

### Lecture 11: Strong Groups vs. Weak Groups
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Identifying weak groups (no base, no eyes, running along the edge).
  * Identifying strong groups (two eyes, thick, influential).
  * The strategy: Use strong groups to attack weak groups.
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "Attacking Weak Groups" by Go Magic (YouTube).
  * *Book:* "Attack and Defense" by Akira Ishida (Chapter 1).

### Lecture 12: Basic Attacking Principles
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The goal of attacking: Not necessarily to kill, but to gain profit elsewhere.
  * How to attack: Drive the weak group toward your strong groups (thickness).
  * Common attacking moves: Knight's move, one-point jump, capping.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Attack and Defense" by Akira Ishida (Chapters 2-4).
  * *Video:* "How to Attack in Go" by Dwyrin (YouTube).

### Lecture 13: Basic Defending and Escaping
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * When to defend vs. when to ignore (tenuki).
  * How to make a base (the vital point of a group).
  * Escaping into the center safely (jumping, making light shape).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Attack and Defense" by Akira Ishida (Chapters on Defense).
  * *Video:* "Defending in Go" by Nicktoof (YouTube).

### Lecture 14: Introduction to Joseki (Corner Formations)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is a joseki? (Locally balanced sequences).
  * Why we play them (efficiency, not memorization).
  * Basic Star Point (4-4) and 3-3 point josekis.
  * The danger of playing joseki without reading the whole board.
* **References:** 
  * *Website:* Josekipedia on GoGameGuru.
  * *Book:* "In the Beginning" by Ishida Yoshio (Chapters on 4-4 and 3-3).

### Lecture 15: Direction of Play
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * "Play away from thickness."
  * Identifying the largest open area on the board.
  * Using your thickness to build a framework (moyo).
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "Direction of Play" by Dwyrin (YouTube).
  * *Book:* "Direction of Play" by Toshiro Kageyama (Introduction and Chapter 1).

### Lecture 16: Basic Proverbs of Go
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * "Urgent moves before big moves."
  * "Hane at the head of two (or three) stones."
  * "Don't play contact Go" (when to attach and when to keep distance).
* **References:** 
  * *Article:* "Go Proverbs" on Sensei's Library.
  * *Book:* "Go Proverbs Illustrated" by D.M. Yang (Select 5-6 proverbs).

### Lecture 17: The Concept of "Aji" (Bad Taste)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is *aji*? (The latent potential or lingering possibilities of stones).
  * Good aji vs. bad aji.
  * How to fix bad aji (consolidating) vs. how to exploit it.
* **References:** 
  * *Article:* "Aji" on Sensei's Library.
  * *Video:* "Understanding Aji" by Go Magic (YouTube).

### Lecture 18: 13x13 Midgame Review and Consolidation
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Reviewing common mistakes made on the 13x13 board.
  * Integrating shape, attack, and direction of play.
  * Transitioning the mindset to the full 19x19 board.
* **References:** 
  * *Activity:* Reviewing student games from the previous weeks. Focus on identifying where the "Direction of Play" was lost.

## Phase 3: Advanced Concepts (19x19 Board)
*Goal: Master the full board. Introduce complex strategic concepts like invasions, reductions, sabaki, and advanced endgame. Play moves to the 19x19 board.*

### Lecture 19: Transition to 19x19 and Full Board Strategy
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * How the 19x19 board changes the pace of the game.
  * Balancing territory and influence on a macro scale.
  * Building a Moyo (framework) and knowing when to cash it in.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "The Dynamics of Go" or general 19x19 strategy videos by Michael Redmond (YouTube).
  * *Video:* "Transitioning to 19x19" by Dwyrin (YouTube).

### Lecture 20: Invasions vs. Reductions
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The difference between invading (living inside) and reducing (limiting from the outside).
  * How to evaluate the opponent's framework to choose the right approach.
  * The risks and rewards of deep invasions.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Invasion and Reduction" by various authors, or chapters in "Opening Theory Made Easy".
  * *Video:* "Invasion vs Reduction" by Go Magic (YouTube).

### Lecture 21: Basic Invasions
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The 3-3 invasion under the 4-4 point.
  * Invading the side (3-4 or 4-3 points).
  * Living locally vs. sacrificing for outside influence.
* **References:** 
  * *Website:* GoGameGuru - "Invasion and Reduction" articles.
  * *Video:* "Basic Invasions" by Nicktoof (YouTube).

### Lecture 22: Basic Reductions
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The shoulder hit (reducing from the outside).
  * Capping and deep reductions.
  * How to handle the opponent's attack after reducing (making light shape).
* **References:** 
  * *Video:* "How to Reduce in Go" by Dwyrin (YouTube).
  * *Book:* "Opening Theory Made Easy" by Otake Hideo (Specific reduction chapters).

### Lecture 23: Sabaki (Making Shape in Enemy Territory)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is *sabaki*? (Flexible, lightweight play in difficult situations).
  * Key techniques: Attaching, hitting, sacrificing stones.
  * The mindset: Don't try to save every stone; aim for a trade.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Sabaki" by Richard Bozulich (Chapters 1-3).
  * *Video:* "Sabaki in Go" by Go Magic (YouTube).

### Lecture 24: Asking Moves and Tesuji Basics
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is an "asking move"? (Forcing the opponent to answer to clarify the position).
  * Basic Tesuji (clever tactical moves): Ladders, nets (geta), snapbacks.
  * How to use asking moves to gain sente or improve your shape.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Tesuji" by James Davies (Introduction and basic chapters).
  * *Video:* "Basic Tesuji" by Nicktoof (YouTube).

### Lecture 25: Using Thickness and Influence Effectively
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The golden rule of thickness: "Do not use thickness to make territory."
  * How to use thickness to attack, reduce, or build a massive framework.
  * Recognizing when thickness has lost its power (over-concentration).
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go" by Toshiro Kageyama (Chapter on Thickness).
  * *Video:* "How to use Thickness" by Dwyrin (YouTube).

### Lecture 26: Deepening Aji and Ko Fights
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * What is a Ko fight? (The rule of Ko, ko threats).
  * How to find and use ko threats.
  * Using bad aji to create ko threats or force the opponent to defend.
* **References:** 
  * *Article:* "Ko Rules" and "Ko Threats" on Sensei's Library.
  * *Video:* "Understanding Ko" by Go Magic (YouTube).

### Lecture 27: Advanced Endgame (Yose) - Sente vs. Gote
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Advanced counting: Double sente, reverse sente.
  * Why reverse sente moves are worth twice as much as normal gote moves.
  * Calculating the exact point value of endgame moves.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "Endgame" by James Davies (Chapters on Sente/Gote endgame).
  * *Video:* "Yose / Endgame Basics" by Michael Redmond (YouTube).

### Lecture 28: Advanced Endgame (Yose) - Common Tesuji
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * The Monkey Jump (Ogeema).
  * The hane, the descent, and the placement.
  * First-line tricks and second-line endgame secrets.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "1000 Life and Death Problems" (Endgame section) or "Yose Fundamentals".
  * *Video:* "Endgame Tesuji" by Nicktoof (YouTube).

### Lecture 29: Game Review and Self-Study Techniques
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * How to review your own games (without AI first, then with AI).
  * Identifying your personal weaknesses (tactics vs. strategy).
  * How to study effectively at the 15k - 10k level.
* **References:** 
  * *Article:* "How to Study Go" on Sensei's Library.
  * *Video:* "How to Review Your Go Games" by Dwyrin (YouTube).

### Lecture 30: The Path to 10k and Beyond (Course Wrap-Up)
* **Table of Contents:** 
  * Summary of the journey from 30k to 10k.
  * The mental aspect of Go: handling losses, patience, and enjoying the game.
  * Setting goals for the next year (reaching 5k).
  * Final Q&A and club tournament announcement.
* **References:** 
  * *Book:* "The Spirit of Go" or general essays by Go professionals on the philosophy of the game.
  * *Activity:* Final club round-robin tournament.

## Instructor Notes for the 4-Hour Meeting Structure:
* **0:00 - 0:20:** The Lecture (Use a physical demonstration board or a projector with a Go client like Sabaki/GoReviewPartner). Keep it interactive; ask the students questions.
* **0:20 - 1:30:** Guided Play & Tsumego. Students play games (starting on 9x9, moving to 13x13, then 19x19 as the year progresses). Beginners who finish early should be given Tsumego puzzles appropriate to their level.
* **1:30 - 2:30:** Game Reviews. Review 2-3 student games. Focus on the specific topic of the day's lecture (e.g., if the lecture was on "Shape", point out bad shapes in their games).
* **2:30 - 3:30:** Free Play / Handicap Games. Allow students to play each other, or play handicap games with the instructor/stronger students.
* **3:30 - 4:00:** Clean up, socializing, and answering individual questions.
