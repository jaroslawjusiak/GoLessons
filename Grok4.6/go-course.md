# One-Year Beginner Go Course Syllabus
**From 30 kyu to approximately 10 kyu**

**Club context:** Weekly 4-hour meetings at a Youth Cultural Centre. Each meeting begins with a 10–20 minute focused lecture, followed by supervised play, game reviews, tsumego (life-and-death problems), and discussion. The course uses 9×9 boards for the first third, transitions through 13×13, and spends the second half on 19×19.

**Overall goals:**
- Master the rules and basic tactics so that games are legally and conceptually correct.
- Develop positional judgment: territory vs. influence, strong vs. weak groups, sente vs. gote.
- Acquire a practical toolkit of shapes, joseki, invasions, reductions, and endgame technique sufficient for solid 10-kyu play.
- Instill good study habits (tsumego, professional game records, self-review).

**Recommended ongoing materials for all students:**
- Sensei’s Library (senseis.xmp.net) – the single most useful free reference.
- “The Second Book of Go” by Richard Bozulich (covers roughly 20–8 kyu).
- “Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go” by Toshiro Kageyama.
- YouTube channels: Nick Sibicky Go Lectures, dwyrin, Michael Redmond 9p.
- Tsumego sources: goproblems.com, TsumegoPro app, “Graded Go Problems for Beginners” (vols. 1–3).

## Phase 1 – Rules and Fundamentals (9×9 board)
*Lectures 1–8*

### Lecture 1: Introduction to Go – History, Equipment and the Spirit of the Game
**Contents:**
- Brief history (China, Korea, Japan) and why we play.
- Board, stones, bowls; how to sit and handle equipment respectfully.
- The object of the game in one sentence: surround more territory than the opponent.
- First placement exercise: empty 9×9, each player puts 5 stones, discuss “feeling.”

**References:**
- Book: Kaoru Iwamoto – *Go for Beginners*, Chapter 1.
- Video: Nick Sibicky Lecture #1 “Introduction to Go”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Go” and “Etiquette”.

### Lecture 2: The Rules – Placing Stones, Capturing and Suicide
**Contents:**
- Alternate turns, black first.
- Liberty and capture (simple examples).
- Suicide is forbidden except when it captures.
- The ko rule (simple recapture prohibition).
- Hands-on: 9×9 capturing race puzzles.

**References:**
- Book: Janice Kim – *Learn to Play Go*, Vol. 1.
- Video: dwyrin “Go Rules Explained in 10 Minutes”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Rules of Go”, “Ko”.

### Lecture 3: Territory, Scoring and the End of the Game
**Contents:**
- What constitutes territory (surrounded empty intersections).
- Japanese vs. Chinese counting (we use Japanese/Ing for the club).
- Dame, seki, and when the game is over.
- Simple 9×9 scoring exercise.

**References:**
- Book: *The Way to Go* by Karl Baker (free PDF), scoring chapter.
- Video: Michael Redmond “How to Count Territory”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Territory”, “Japanese Counting”.

### Lecture 4: Life and Death – Two Eyes Make a Group Immortal
**Contents:**
- The concept of an eye.
- Two real eyes = living group.
- False eyes and how they fail.
- Basic living shapes (straight four, bent four in the corner, etc.).

**References:**
- Book: James Davies – *Life and Death* (Elementary Go Series).
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Life and Death Basics”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Two Eyes”, “False Eye”.

### Lecture 5: Killing Groups – Shortage of Liberties and Connecting
**Contents:**
- Atari and double atari.
- Connecting stones to gain liberties.
- Cutting to separate groups.
- Simple “kill or live” problems on 9×9.

**References:**
- Book: *Graded Go Problems for Beginners*, Vol. 1.
- Video: dwyrin “How to Kill a Group”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Capturing”, “Cutting”.

### Lecture 6: Basic Tactics – Ladders, Nets and Snapbacks
**Contents:**
- The ladder (shicho) and ladder-breaker.
- The net (geta).
- Snapback (uttegae).
- When to use each technique.

**References:**
- Book: James Davies – *Tesuji*.
- Video: Nick Sibicky Lecture on Ladders.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Ladder”, “Geta”, “Snapback”.

### Lecture 7: Connecting, Cutting and Elementary Shapes on 9×9
**Contents:**
- Solid connection vs. diagonal vs. one-point jump.
- The empty triangle (bad shape).
- Tiger’s mouth and table shape (good).
- Mini-games focused only on connection/cut.

**References:**
- Book: Kageyama – *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go*, shape chapters.
- Video: dwyrin “Good Shape vs Bad Shape”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Shape”, “Empty Triangle”.

### Lecture 8: Playing and Reviewing Complete 9×9 Games + Ko Fights
**Contents:**
- Full even-game 9×9 (komi 5.5 or 6.5).
- How to review: “Why did this group die?”, “Was this move big?”
- Ko fights: ko threats and when to ignore.
- Transition talk: why we now move to 13×13.

**References:**
- Book: *The Second Book of Go*, Chapter 1.
- Video: any 9×9 game review by Michael Redmond or Andrew Jackson.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “9x9”, “Ko Threat”.

## Phase 2 – Strategic Foundations (13×13 board)
*Lectures 9–16*

### Lecture 9: Why Larger Boards? Corners, Sides and Centre
**Contents:**
- Efficiency: corners are easiest to surround, then sides, then centre.
- The 3-3, 3-4, 4-4 points.
- First 10 moves of a 13×13 game – typical patterns.
- Direction of play on a small board.

**References:**
- Book: Ikuro Ishigure – *In the Beginning*.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Opening Principles”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Corner”, “Side”, “Center”.

### Lecture 10: Opening Fundamentals – Enclosures and Extensions
**Contents:**
- Shimari (corner enclosure): small knight, large knight, one-point.
- Extensions along the side (two-space, three-space).
- Approach moves (kakari) and simple answers.
- 13×13 even-game demonstration.

**References:**
- Book: Otake Hideo – *Opening Theory Made Easy* (first chapters).
- Video: dwyrin “How to Open on 13×13”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Shimari”, “Extension”.

### Lecture 11: Territory versus Influence – Keeping the Balance
**Contents:**
- Territory = concrete points now.
- Influence (thickness) = potential and attacking power.
- Over-concentrated vs. over-extended positions.
- Simple examples of converting influence into territory.

**References:**
- Book: Kageyama – *Lessons in the Fundamentals*, “Thickness”.
- Video: Michael Redmond “Territory and Influence”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Territory versus Influence”.

### Lecture 12: Strong Groups and Weak Groups
**Contents:**
- What makes a group strong (eyes, thickness, connection to friendly stones).
- Weak groups invite attack.
- Over-concentration (too many stones doing the same job).
- How to treat a weak group: run, settle, or sacrifice.

**References:**
- Book: *Attack and Defense* by Ishida & Davies, early chapters.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Strong and Weak Groups”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Weak Group”, “Thickness”.

### Lecture 13: Attack and Defense
**Contents:**
- The purpose of attack is profit, not necessarily capture.
- Capping, pressing, splitting.
- Defending by making eyes, connecting, or counter-attacking.
- 13×13 fighting examples.

**References:**
- Book: *Attack and Defense* (Elementary Go Series).
- Video: dwyrin “How to Attack”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Attack”, “Defense”.

### Lecture 14: Good Shapes and Bad Shapes
**Contents:**
- Table shape, tiger mouth, hanging connection.
- Empty triangle, dumpling, clumsy diagonal.
- “Hane at the head of two (or three) stones.”
- Shape problems on 13×13.

**References:**
- Book: Kageyama – shape chapters (the whole book is about this).
- Video: dwyrin “Shape Lecture Series”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Good Shape”, “Bad Shape”.

### Lecture 15: Sente and Gote – The Initiative
**Contents:**
- Sente = a move the opponent must answer.
- Gote = a move that loses the initiative.
- Double sente, reverse sente.
- When it is correct to ignore a gote move.

**References:**
- Book: *The Second Book of Go*, sente/gote chapter.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Sente and Gote”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Sente”, “Gote”.

### Lecture 16: Basic Fighting – Capturing Races and Cutting Battles
**Contents:**
- Semeai (capturing races) – counting liberties.
- Inside liberties vs. outside liberties.
- When to cut and when to connect.
- Transition talk: we now move to the full 19×19 board.

**References:**
- Book: James Davies – *Tesuji* and *Life and Death*.
- Video: Michael Redmond “Capturing Races”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Semeai”, “Liberty”.

## Phase 3 – Full-Board Strategy (19×19 board)
*Lectures 17–30*

### Lecture 17: The 19×19 Board – Fuseki and Direction of Play
**Contents:**
- Star points, 3-4, 4-4, 3-3, 5-3, 5-4.
- Chinese, Kobayashi, Sanrensei, mini-Chinese openings (very light introduction).
- “Play in the biggest area” and “direction of play.”
- First 20 moves of a professional game.

**References:**
- Book: *In the Beginning* + *Opening Theory Made Easy*.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “19×19 Opening”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Fuseki”, “Direction of Play”.

### Lecture 18: Invasions – When and How
**Contents:**
- 3-3 invasion of a 4-4 stone.
- Invading a one-space enclosure.
- Timing: too early vs. too late.
- Living small vs. causing damage.

**References:**
- Book: *Invasion and Reduction* (or relevant chapters in *Second Book of Go*).
- Video: dwyrin “3-3 Invasion”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Invasion”, “3-3 Invasion”.

### Lecture 19: Reductions and Moyo
**Contents:**
- Reducing a large framework instead of invading.
- Shoulder hit, capping play, erasing.
- How big a moyo needs to be before it is invaded.
- Converting moyo into definite territory.

**References:**
- Book: *Strategic Concepts of Go* by Nagahara.
- Video: Michael Redmond “Reducing Moyo”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Moyo”, “Reduction”.

### Lecture 20: Using Influence and Thickness
**Contents:**
- Thickness looking toward the centre or toward a weak group.
- “Don’t make territory from thickness – attack with it.”
- Examples of thickness that became useless (over-concentrated).
- Practical 19×19 examples.

**References:**
- Book: Kageyama – thickness chapters.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Thickness and Influence”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Thickness”.

### Lecture 21: Efficiency of Moves – Avoiding Waste
**Contents:**
- Over-concentration revisited on the big board.
- “One stone, two purposes.”
- Playing too close to thickness.
- Counting the value of a move (very roughly).

**References:**
- Book: *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go*.
- Video: dwyrin “Efficient Moves”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Efficiency”, “Overconcentration”.

### Lecture 22: Essential Go Proverbs
**Contents:**
- “Urgent points before big points.”
- “Hane at the head of two/three stones.”
- “Don’t go fishing when your house is on fire.”
- “The enemy’s key point is your own.”
- “On the third line, six die, eight live” (and other side-proverbs).
- How to apply them in real games.

**References:**
- Book: *Go Proverbs Illustrated* by Segoe Kensaku (or any proverb collection).
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Go Proverbs”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Proverbs”.

### Lecture 23: Simple Joseki for 10-kyu Players
**Contents:**
- 4-4 point joseki (knight approach, one-space high, 3-3).
- 3-4 point joseki (small knight approach, two-space high).
- Joseki is a toolbox, not a ritual – when to deviate.
- Common joseki mistakes at our level.

**References:**
- Book: *38 Basic Joseki* (Elementary Go Series) – selected joseki only.
- Video: dwyrin or Haylee “Beginner Joseki”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Joseki”, specific pages for 4-4 and 3-4.

### Lecture 24: Aji – Hidden Potential
**Contents:**
- What aji is (lingering possibilities).
- Good aji vs. bad aji.
- Leaving aji for later vs. cleaning it up.
- Using aji to live or to destroy.

**References:**
- Book: *The Second Book of Go*, aji chapter.
- Video: Michael Redmond “Aji”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Aji”.

### Lecture 25: Kikashi (Forcing Moves) and Basic Tesuji
**Contents:**
- Asking moves that must be answered.
- Typical tesuji: tesuji for capturing, for living, for connecting.
- When a kikashi becomes aji.
- Short tesuji problems.

**References:**
- Book: James Davies – *Tesuji*.
- Video: Nick Sibicky “Tesuji”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Kikashi”, “Tesuji”.

### Lecture 26: Sabaki – Light and Flexible Play
**Contents:**
- Making a group light so it can be sacrificed if necessary.
- The “skipping stone” and “diagonal” techniques.
- Sabaki vs. heavy play.
- Examples of living in the opponent’s sphere of influence.

**References:**
- Book: *Attack and Defense* and Kageyama.
- Video: dwyrin “Sabaki”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Sabaki”, “Light”, “Heavy”.

### Lecture 27: Yose – The Endgame
**Contents:**
- Counting the size of endgame moves.
- Sente yose vs. gote yose vs. reverse sente.
- Common 2-point, 3-point, 4-point moves.
- How to practise counting.

**References:**
- Book: *Yose* (Elementary Go Series) or Ogawa & Davies *The Endgame*.
- Video: Michael Redmond “Endgame”.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Yose”, “Endgame”.

### Lecture 28: Life and Death and Semeai at a Higher Level
**Contents:**
- Common 10-kyu tsumego (corner positions, shortage of liberties).
- Capturing races with large numbers of liberties.
- Throw-ins, placing stones inside eyes, etc.
- Practical “this group must live / must die” in 19×19 games.

**References:**
- Book: *Graded Go Problems for Beginners* Vol. 3 + *Life and Death*.
- Video: any tsumego series by Nick Sibicky or GoMagic.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Tsumego”, “Semeai”.

### Lecture 29: Middle-Game Strategy – Splitting, Fighting and Planning
**Contents:**
- Splitting attacks (dividing two weak groups).
- Choosing which fight to start.
- When to play quietly and when to fight.
- Whole-board thinking vs. local fighting.

**References:**
- Book: *Strategic Concepts of Go* + *Attack and Defense*.
- Video: Nick Sibicky middle-game lectures.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “Middle Game”, “Splitting Attack”.

### Lecture 30: How to Keep Improving – Review, Study and the Road to 10 kyu (and beyond)
**Contents:**
- How to review your own games (alone and with a stronger player).
- Recommended study diet: 30 % tsumego, 30 % professional games, 40 % playing.
- Online servers (OGS, KGS, Fox, Tygem) and etiquette.
- Setting a realistic goal: 10 kyu in 12 months, then 5 kyu, etc.
- Club next steps: teaching the next generation of beginners.

**References:**
- Book: *The Second Book of Go* (final chapters) + Kageyama (whole book reread).
- Video: “How to Study Go” talks by Michael Redmond, Andrew Jackson, or BenKyo.
- Article: Sensei’s Library – “How to Study”, “Improving”.

---

**Practical notes for the teacher:**
- After every lecture, students play at least two games on the current board size; the teacher reviews one or two on a demonstration board.
- Keep a shared club notebook of “proverb of the week” and “shape of the week.”
- From Lecture 17 onward, show one professional game (or a fragment) each week, focusing only on the topic just taught.
- Encourage students to do 5–10 tsumego every day at home.

This syllabus is a living document; feel free to spend two weeks on a difficult topic (life-and-death, sente/gote, yose) if the group needs it. The important thing is that by the end of the year the students can play a reasonably coherent 19×19 game, recognise the main strategic concepts, and know how to continue studying on their own.
