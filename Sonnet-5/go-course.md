# Go Course for Beginners → Intermediate Player
### A 30-Meeting Syllabus (One Academic Year, Weekly Meetings, ~4 hours each)

---

## Course Overview

**Goal:** Take a complete beginner (30 kyu) to a solid intermediate level (~10 kyu) over one year of weekly meetings.

**Structure of each meeting (suggested, ~4 hours):**
- 10–20 min: Lecture (topic below)
- 60–90 min: Guided exercises / problems related to the lecture
- Remaining time: Supervised practice games + review/teaching games with instructor

**Board progression:**
- **Meetings 1–5:** 9×9 board (rules, tactics, life & death basics)
- **Meetings 6–15:** 13×13 board (strategy, shape, opening principles)
- **Meetings 16–30:** 19×19 board (full strategy, joseki, middle game, endgame)

**General reference resources (useful throughout the whole course):**
- [Sensei's Library](https://senseis.xmp.net/) – community Go wiki, great for looking up any term
- [OGS – Online-Go.com](https://online-go.com/learn-to-play-go) – free interactive "Learn to Play Go" tutorial
- [Nick Sibicky's Go Lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLYnp8BiuGVN0dQTAXCq2Pi9nWTKzQEPss) – Seattle Go Center, huge free lecture library (great for lectures 15+)
- [Dwyrin's Go Lessons](https://www.youtube.com/@Dwyrin) – YouTube, beginner/intermediate friendly
- [InSeong's Go Dojang](https://www.youtube.com/@InSeongsGoDojang) – excellent structured lectures
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go* series (vols. 1–5) – the classic beginner book series
- [British Go Association – Learning Resources](https://www.britgo.org/learning)

## PART 1 — Foundations on the 9×9 Board (Meetings 1–5)

### Meeting 1: What is Go? Rules, Equipment, and First Moves
- History and philosophy of Go (brief)
- Board, stones, equipment; how to hold/place stones
- Basic rules: turns, liberties, capturing, no-suicide rule
- Goal of the game: surrounding territory
- Play first full games on 9×9 with instructor supervision
**References:**
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 1*
- [OGS Interactive Tutorial](https://online-go.com/learn-to-play-go)
- [AGA "Learn to Play" page](https://www.usgo.org/learn-to-play-go/)

### Meeting 2: Liberties, Atari, and Capturing
- Counting liberties
- Atari — recognizing and responding
- Simple captures, the ladder (shicho) technique
- Introduction to the "net" (geta) capture
**References:**
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 1* (capturing chapters)
- [Sensei's Library: Ladder](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Ladder)
- Video: "Go Basics: Ladders and Nets" — search Dwyrin's channel

### Meeting 3: Territory, Scoring, and Neutral Points
- What counts as territory
- Area scoring vs. territory scoring (Chinese vs Japanese rules)
- Dame (neutral points)
- Ending a game — when to pass
- Practice: counting finished 9×9 games
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Scoring](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Scoring)
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 1*

### Meeting 4: Life and Death — The Concept of Two Eyes
- Why one eye is not enough
- Recognizing living shapes vs dead shapes
- Simple eye-shapes (straight three, bent shapes)
- Basic false eye concept
**References:**
- James Davies, *Life and Death* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 3) — first chapters
- [Sensei's Library: Two Eyes](https://senseis.xmp.net/?TwoEyes)

### Meeting 5: Connections, Cuts, and Introduction to Ko
- Solid connection vs. diagonal connection
- Cutting points — why they matter
- What is Ko? Basic ko rule and ko threats (intro only)
- Review game: full 9×9 game with commentary
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Ko](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Ko)
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 2* (early chapters)

## PART 2 — Building Strategy on the 13×13 Board (Meetings 6–15)

### Meeting 6: Opening Principles — Corners, Sides, Center
- Why corners are easiest to secure, sides next, center hardest
- Value of the first moves (opening priorities)
- Introduction to the concept of "big points"
**References:**
- Otake Hideo, *Opening Theory Made Easy*
- [Sensei's Library: Fuseki](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Fuseki)

### Meeting 7: Strong Groups vs. Weak Groups
- What makes a group "strong" (eye shape, space, support)
- Recognizing weak groups — your own and opponent's
- Basic principle: don't create unnecessary weak groups
**References:**
- Ishida & Davies, *Attack and Defense* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 5), intro chapters

### Meeting 8: Introduction to Shape — Good Shape vs. Bad Shape
- The empty triangle (why it's bad)
- Good shapes: the ponnuki, the tiger's mouth
- Why shape efficiency matters
**References:**
- Toshiro Kageyama, *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* — Chapter on shape
- Charles Matthews, *Making Good Shape* (Kiseido)

### Meeting 9: Attack and Defense Basics
- How to attack without overplaying
- How to defend efficiently
- Attacking to build strength elsewhere (not to kill)
**References:**
- Ishida & Davies, *Attack and Defense*
- Nick Sibicky lecture: "Attacking Weak Groups"

### Meeting 10: Sente and Gote
- Definition of sente (initiative) and gote (loss of initiative)
- Why sente is valuable
- Recognizing sente/gote moves in a sequence
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Sente](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Sente)
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 3*

### Meeting 11: Introduction to Joseki — Simple Corner Patterns
- What is joseki and why it exists
- 2–3 very simple corner sequences (star point approach, 3-3 invasion basics)
- Choosing joseki based on the whole board (brief intro)
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Joseki](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Joseki)
- [josekipedia.com](http://www.josekipedia.com/) (interactive joseki dictionary)

### Meeting 12: Basic Tesuji — Clever Tactical Moves
- The concept of tesuji vs. brute force
- Simple tesujis: the hane, the block, the "crosscut" response
- Introductory tesuji problems
**References:**
- James Davies, *Tesuji* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 2)

### Meeting 13: Cutting Strategy and Splitting Groups
- When and why to cut
- Peeps and their purpose
- How cutting relates to attack
**References:**
- Ishida & Davies, *Attack and Defense*, cutting chapters

### Meeting 14: Direction of Play (Introduction)
- Choosing which side to block/extend based on surrounding stones
- Thickness — what it is and how to use it (avoid neighboring thickness)
- Simple whole-board judgment exercises
**References:**
- Takeo Kajiwara, *The Direction of Play*
- [Sensei's Library: Thickness](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Thickness)

### Meeting 15: Review and Transition — Playing on the Full Board
- Recap of all 13×13 concepts
- Introduction to the 19×19 board — scale and pacing differences
- How opening priorities shift with more space
- First full 19×19 practice game (with heavy handicap/guidance)
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: BoardSize](https://senseis.xmp.net/?BoardSize)
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 4*

## PART 3 — Advanced Concepts on the 19×19 Board (Meetings 16–30)

### Meeting 16: Fuseki Theory — Whole Board Openings
- Common opening patterns (Chinese, Sanren-sei, low/high approaches)
- Balance between speed and stability
- Territory vs. influence-oriented openings (intro)
**References:**
- Otake Hideo, *Opening Theory Made Easy*
- Kim & Jeong, *Fundamental Principles of Go*

### Meeting 17: Influence vs. Territory — Finding Balance
- Concrete territory vs. potential (moyo)
- How to evaluate a position with mixed influence/territory
- Introductory moyo-building and moyo-breaking ideas
**References:**
- Takeo Kajiwara, *The Direction of Play*
- [Sensei's Library: Moyo](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Moyo)

### Meeting 18: Go Proverbs I — Shape and Local Tactics
- "Hane at the head of two/three stones"
- "Don't crawl on the second line"
- "Ponnuki is worth 30 points"
- Practice identifying proverbs in real game positions
**References:**
- *Go Proverbs* (Milton Bradley/Elwyn Berlekamp, Ishi Press)
- [Sensei's Library: Go Proverbs list](https://senseis.xmp.net/?GoProverbs)

### Meeting 19: Go Proverbs II — Strategic Priorities
- "Urgent moves before big moves"
- "Play away from strength"
- "Lose your first game of Go as quickly as possible" (learning attitude)
- Applying proverbs to whole-board judgment exercises
**References:**
- *Go Proverbs* (Berlekamp)
- Nick Sibicky lecture series on strategy

### Meeting 20: Joseki Revisited — Choosing the Right Variation
- How joseki choice depends on the whole board (not memorization)
- Common mistakes: playing joseki "on autopilot"
- 3–4 intermediate joseki with alternatives
**References:**
- Yilun Yang, *Whole Board Thinking in Joseki* (2 vols.)
- [josekipedia.com](http://www.josekipedia.com/)

### Meeting 21: Invasions — Entering Enemy Territory
- When invasion is justified vs. reckless
- Common invasion points (3-3, corner points in large frameworks)
- Basic invasion follow-up (making a base)
**References:**
- Yuan Zhou, *Reducing Territorial Frameworks*
- Nick Sibicky lecture: "Invasions"

### Meeting 22: Reduction — Attacking Frameworks from Outside
- Difference between invasion and reduction
- Choosing reduction points
- Light approach vs. heavy approach
**References:**
- Yuan Zhou, *Reducing Territorial Frameworks*
- [Sensei's Library: Reduction](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Reduction)

### Meeting 23: Sabaki — Making Light, Flexible Shape
- Definition of sabaki and when it's needed (weak group in enemy sphere)
- Sacrificing stones for speed/shape
- Sabaki vs. simply running away
**References:**
- Cho Chikun, *Sabaki* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 7)
- Nick Sibicky lecture: "Sabaki Techniques"

### Meeting 24: Aji — Latent Potential in the Position
- What is aji, and why not to "use it up" too early
- Aji-keshi — the mistake of destroying your own aji
- Examples of dormant aji becoming useful later in the game
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Aji](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Aji)
- Yuan Zhou, *Cosmic Go* (aji-related tactics)

### Meeting 25: Kikashi — Asking Moves
- What makes a move a "forcing move" (kikashi)
- Difference between kikashi and simple sente
- Recognizing when a stone is kikashi vs. a wasted move
**References:**
- [Sensei's Library: Kikashi](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Kikashi)
- Nick Sibicky lecture: "Forcing Moves"

### Meeting 26: Middle Game Strategy — Whole Board Thinking
- Evaluating multiple weak groups on the board
- Prioritizing between attack, defense, and territory
- Efficiency of moves — the concept of "wasted moves"
**References:**
- Kageyama, *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go*
- Ishida & Davies, *Attack and Defense*

### Meeting 27: Life and Death — Intermediate Problems
- Common corner life-and-death shapes (six die, bulky five, etc.)
- Key point (vital point) identification
- Timed problem-solving session
**References:**
- Richard Bozulich, *Graded Go Problems for Beginners, Vol. 3–4*
- James Davies, *Life and Death*

### Meeting 28: Introduction to Yose (Endgame) — Types of Endgame Moves
- Double sente, sente, gote endgame moves
- Estimating point value of endgame plays
- Order of endgame play
**References:**
- Ogawa & Davies, *Endgame* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 9)
- [Sensei's Library: Yose](https://senseis.xmp.net/?Yose)

### Meeting 29: Advanced Yose — Counting and Sente/Gote Sequencing
- How to count territory differences precisely
- Practice: sequencing a list of endgame moves by value
- Common endgame tesuji (monkey jump, hane-connect, etc.)
**References:**
- Ogawa & Davies, *Endgame*
- Nick Sibicky lecture: "Endgame Fundamentals"

### Meeting 30: Course Review, Tournament Preparation, and Further Study
- Recap of the year: key concepts revisited through a full game review
- Playing etiquette, tournament rules, using clocks
- How to continue improving: recommended books, online servers (OGS, KGS, Fox), local/regional tournaments
- Group tournament / final full-board game with instructor commentary
**References:**
- [European Go Federation – Tournament Calendar](https://www.eurogofed.org/)
- [American Go Association](https://www.usgo.org/)
- Janice Kim, *Learn to Play Go, Vol. 5* (Haengma) — for continued study
- Kim & Jeong, *Fundamental Principles of Go* — for continued study

## Suggested Take-Home Practice Throughout the Course
- **Problem books:** Richard Bozulich, *Graded Go Problems for Beginners* (Vols. 1–4) — assign progressively as the course advances
- **Online play:** Encourage students to play on [OGS](https://online-go.com) or [KGS](http://www.gokgs.com) between meetings, and to use built-in AI review tools (e.g., KataGo via OGS) to check their games
- **Game review habit:** Teach students early to review their own games after every session — this is one of the fastest ways to improve
