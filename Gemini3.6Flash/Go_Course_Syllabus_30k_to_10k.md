# One-Year Go Course Syllabus: From 30 Kyu to 10 Kyu

**Target Audience:** Absolute beginners to intermediate players  
**Setting:** Local Youth Cultural Centre Go Club  
**Format:** Weekly 4-hour sessions (15–20 minute lecture + SGF review / guided practice / rated games)  
**Total Duration:** 30 Weeks (3 Phases: 9x9, 13x13, 19x19)

---

## Course Overview & Objectives

This syllabus provides a structured, step-by-step curriculum designed to guide complete beginners (30 kyu) to a solid intermediate club level (10 kyu) over the course of 30 weekly meetings.

### Key Milestones:
*   **Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): 9x9 Board — Fundamental Rules & Life/Death Basics**
    *   *Goal:* Understand liberties, capturing, two eyes, basic life and death, and 9x9 tactical completion (30k → 22k).
*   **Phase 2 (Weeks 5–14): 13x13 Board — Opening Strategy, Shapes & Fundamental Tactics**
    *   *Goal:* Transition to full-board awareness, understand opening principles (Corners → Sides → Center), basic tactical tesuji, sente/gote, and strong vs. weak groups (22k → 15k).
*   **Phase 3 (Weeks 15–30): 19x19 Board — Advanced Strategy, Direction of Play, Sabaki & Endgame**
    *   *Goal:* Master full-scale 19x19 strategic concepts, direction of play, joseki frameworks, using influence, aji, sabaki, and systematic endgame calculation (15k → 10k).

## Phase 1: The 9x9 Board — Fundamentals & Rules (Weeks 1–4)

### Week 1: Introduction to Go — Rules, Liberties, and Capturing
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   The origin and beauty of Go.
    *   Intersections, turns, placing stones on lines.
    *   Liberties (Breathing points) of a single stone and connected groups.
    *   Atari (Immediate threat of capture) and capturing stones.
    *   The rule of suicide (Illegal moves) and the Ko rule (avoiding infinite loops - basic introduction).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Janice Kim & Jeong Soo-hyun, *Learn to Play Go: Volume 1 - The Game of Go*.
    *   *Video:* In Sente, *Go Lessons for Beginners - Lesson 1: Rules & Capturing* (YouTube).
    *   *Interactive:* [Online-Go.com (OGG) Interactive Tutorial](https://online-go.com/learn-to-play-go).

---

### Week 2: Territory, Scoring, and Knowing When the Game Ends
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is territory? Surrounded empty intersections vs. captures.
    *   Surrounding territory efficiently on 9x9.
    *   Passing rules and agreeing when the game is over.
    *   Basic counting (Japanese scoring fundamentals).
    *   Komi (Compensation for playing second).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Yasutoshi Miyamoto, *The Rules of Go*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *How to Play Go - Chapter 2: Territory and Counting* (YouTube/Go Magic).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Territory and Scoring for Beginners*.

---

### Week 3: Immortal Groups — The Concept of Two Eyes & False Eyes
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Why single-space eyes cannot be captured.
    *   The definition of an "unconditional life": Two independent eyes.
    *   Recognizing "False Eyes" (Eyes that can be collapsed).
    *   How to make two eyes in tight quarters on 9x9.
    *   Capturing dead groups inside your territory.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* James Davies, *Life and Death* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 4).
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Go Lecture 1: Two Eyes and Basic Life/Death* (YouTube).
    *   *App/Site:* [Tsumego Hero](https://tsumego-hero.com/) (Beginner Life & Death Sets).

---

### Week 4: Basic Life & Death Patterns — Vital Points & Nakade
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Common dead and alive shapes (Straight 3, Bent 3, Square 4, Pyramid 4, Bulky 5).
    *   Finding the "Vital Point" (The spot that grants 2 eyes or denies them).
    *   "Nakade" (Playing inside an opponent's eye-space to kill).
    *   Basic ladder (*shicho*) and net (*geta*) capturing techniques on 9x9.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Cho Chikun, *Elementary Go Problems (Vol. 1)* / *All About Life and Death*.
    *   *Video:* Michael Redmond 9p, *Beginner Life and Death Masterclass* (YouTube - AGA Channel).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Nakade / Basic Eye Shapes*.

---

## Phase 2: The 13x13 Board — Strategy, Shapes & Transition (Weeks 5–14)

### Week 5: Transitioning to 13x13 — Corners, Sides, and Center Efficiency
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Moving from 9x9 to 13x13: Why scale changes strategy.
    *   The golden rule of spatial efficiency: Corners > Sides > Center.
    *   Why corners require fewer stones to enclose territory (The Gold, Silver, Grass analogy).
    *   Basic corner enclosure (*shimari*) and corner approach (*kakari*).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Otake Hideo, *Opening Theory Made Easy*.
    *   *Video:* Dwyer (Clossius), *Basic Opening Strategy - Corners First* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Corner, Side, Center*.

---

### Week 6: Good Shapes vs. Bad Shapes — Building Efficient Frameworks
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is shape in Go? Connections, lightness, and efficiency.
    *   Good shapes: Bamboo joint (*takefu*), One-space jump (*ikken-tobi*), Knight's move (*keima*), Mouth shape.
    *   Bad shapes: Empty triangle (*akatriangle*), Dango (clumped stones), Pushing along the second line.
    *   Why bad shapes waste liberties and efficiency.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Kageyama Toshiro, *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* (Chapter: "Good Shape and Bad Shape").
    *   *Video:* In Sente, *Shape Principles in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Good Shape / Bad Shape*.

---

### Week 7: Sente and Gote — Maintaining the Initiative
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Defining *Sente* (Initiative / Forcing move) vs. *Gote* (Losing initiative / Passive answer).
    *   Why Sente is worth double: Dictating the flow of the game.
    *   Recognizing absolute forcing moves vs. optional responses.
    *   How to steal Sente from your opponent (*Tenuki* - playing elsewhere).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Cho Chikun, *Go: A Complete Introduction to the Game*.
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Lecture on Sente, Gote, and Tenuki* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Sente and Gote*.

---

### Week 8: Strong Groups vs. Weak Groups — Stability & Attack Target
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   How to evaluate group stability: Base (eye space) + Escape routes.
    *   What makes a group "weak"? (No base + surrounded).
    *   Rule #1 of Go: "Settle your weak groups before attacking."
    *   Using weak opponent groups to build territory naturally.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Rin Kaiho, *Dictionary of Basic Strategy* / *Fundamental Principles of Go*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *Weak and Strong Groups Explained* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Strong and Weak Groups*.

---

### Week 9: Tactical Tesuji I — Cutting, Connecting, and Peeping
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is a *Tesuji*? (A clever tactical technique).
    *   Cutting techniques: Cross-cut, wedge (*wari-komi*), snapback.
    *   Connecting techniques: Solid connect, one-space jump connection, bamboo joint.
    *   The "Peep" (*nozoki*): Forcing opponent into heavy/bad shapes. Rule: "Don't push through a knight's move unless cutting."
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* James Davies, *Tesuji* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 3).
    *   *Video:* Shawn Ray (Clossius), *Fundamental Tesuji Patterns* (YouTube).
    *   *App:* [BadukPop](https://badukpop.com/) (Tactical Tesuji Drills).

---

### Week 10: Tactical Tesuji II — Esoteric Capturing Techniques
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Advanced ladder breakers and reading ladders (*shicho*).
    *   The Crane's Nest tesuji (*Tsuru no Sugomori*).
    *   Under the Stones (*Ishi-no-Shita*) preview.
    *   Loose net (*geta*) and loose ladder techniques.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Fujisawa Shukai, *Tesuji Power Builder*.
    *   *Video:* Michael Redmond 9p, *Classic Tesuji Patterns* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Capturing Tesuji*.

---

### Week 11: Principles of Attack — Surrounding, Not Killing
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   The core misconception: Attacking is NOT for killing!
    *   Attacking to build profit (territory or thickness) elsewhere.
    *   Attacking from a distance (don't touch what you attack).
    *   Leaning attacks (*motsure*): Attacking group A to build power against group B.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Kato Masao, *Attack and Defense* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 5).
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Lecture 42: How to Attack Properly* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Attack and Defense Principles*.

---

### Week 12: Principles of Defense — Making Base & Escaping to the Center
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Defensive strategy 1: Slide to the 2nd/3rd line to make a rapid eye base.
    *   Defensive strategy 2: Jump out to the center when surrounded on sides.
    *   When to sacrifice useless stones (*捨て石 - Suteishi*).
    *   Avoiding heavy, helpless groups.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Kato Masao, *Attack and Defense* (Chapters on Defense).
    *   *Video:* In Sente, *Defensive Shapes and Base Making* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Making a Base*.

---

### Week 13: Territory vs. Influence — The Thick vs. Thin Paradigm
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is "Influence" (*Atsumi* / Thickness)?
    *   Why influence is not territory, but potential power.
    *   How to build wall structures (thickness) without making eye spaces directly.
    *   The crucial principle: "Do not use thickness to make territory directly; use thickness to attack."
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Ishida Yoshio, *All About Thickness*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *Influence vs Territory in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Thickness*.

---

### Week 14: Introduction to Endgame (Yose) on 13x13
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is *Yose* (Endgame)? Transitioning from midgame to boundary closing.
    *   Calculating point values of boundary moves (Sente yose vs. Double Gote).
    *   2nd-line boundary plays: Monkey jump (*saru-suberi*), hane and connect.
    *   The golden rule of Yose: Play Double Sente → Sente → Reverse Sente → Gote.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Tomoko Ogawa & James Davies, *The Endgame* (Elementary Go Series, Vol. 6).
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Intro to Endgame Calculations* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Endgame Value Calculations*.

---

## Phase 3: The 19x19 Board — Full Strategy & Advanced Concepts (Weeks 15–30)

### Week 15: Transitioning to 19x19 — Full-Board Opening Frameworks (*Fuseki*)
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Moving to the standard 19x19 grid: Scale and patience.
    *   Classic opening structures: Star point (*Hoshi*), 3-4 point (*Komoku*), 3-5 point (*Mokuhazu*).
    *   High vs. Low placement (3rd line = territory, 4th line = influence).
    *   Popular Fuseki formations: Parallel opening, Sanrensei (Three star points), Chinese Opening.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Sakata Eio, *Modern Opening Strategy* / Otake Hideo, *Opening Theory Made Easy*.
    *   *Video:* Michael Redmond 9p, *Basic Fuseki Patterns on 19x19* (YouTube - AGA).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Fuseki*.

---

### Week 16: Essential Star-Point (*Hoshi*) Joseki in the AI Era
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is *Joseki*? (Balanced local sequences in the corner).
    *   The classic 3-6 / knight's approach to the Star point.
    *   Modern AI-inspired 3-3 point early invasion vs. attachment Joseki.
    *   Why Joseki is not memorization, but local balance and shape.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Dai Junfu, *Essential Joseki for Beginners*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *Modern Star Point Joseki Guide* (YouTube).
    *   *Website:* [Josekipedia](http://www.josekipedia.com/) / [OGG Joseki Dictionary](https://online-go.com/joseki).

---

### Week 17: Essential 3-4 Point (*Komoku*) Joseki
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Characteristics of the 3-4 point (asymmetrical, territory-oriented).
    *   Low approach (*keima-gakari*) and High approach (*ikken-gakari*).
    *   Pincer tactics (*hasami*): 1-space, 2-space, and 3-space pincers.
    *   Choosing a Joseki that matches surrounding global stones.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Ishida Yoshio, *Dictionary of Basic Joseki (Vol. 1)*.
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Basic Komoku Joseki Explained* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Komoku Joseki*.

---

### Week 18: Direction of Play — Where to Play Next?
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Evaluating the biggest areas of the board.
    *   Developing from your widest side (*Moyo* building).
    *   Approaching from the wider side vs. enclosing your own corner.
    *   Preventing opponent's big frameworks before they become unmanageable.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Takeo Kajiwara, *The Direction of Play*.
    *   *Video:* Clossius, *How to Find the Biggest Move on the Board* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Direction of Play*.

---

### Week 19: Famous Go Proverbs & How to Apply Them
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   "Urgent moves before big moves" (Safety before greed).
    *   "Hane at the head of two (or three) stones" (Head-slap pattern for power).
    *   "Play away from strength" (Don't approach strong walls).
    *   "My opponent's vital point is my vital point."
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Radagast & Sensei's Library Contributors, *Go Proverbs*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *10 Essential Go Proverbs Every Player Must Know* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Go Proverbs Index*.

---

### Week 20: Invasions — Breaking Opponent's Territory
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   When is a framework (*moyo*) ripe for invasion?
    *   Standard invasion points: 3-3 corner invasions, side-extensions invasions.
    *   Finding the extension limit: The "N + 1" rule for side extensions.
    *   Creating a living group inside enemy territory without ruining your own game.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Cho Chikun, *Reducing and Invading Frameworks*.
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *When and How to Invade* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Invasion*.

---

### Week 21: Reductions — Lightness and Capping Moves
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Invasion vs. Reduction: Knowing when *not* to invade deep.
    *   The "Boundary Line" (Line of symmetry between opposing frameworks).
    *   The Capping Move (*boushi*): Erasing moyos from the outside.
    *   Playing "lightly" (*karui*): Being willing to discard single stones to trim enemy points.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Cho Chikun, *Reducing and Invading Frameworks*.
    *   *Video:* Michael Redmond 9p, *Reducing Moyos: Capping & Shoulder Hits* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Reduction*.

---

### Week 22: Sabaki — Settling Weak Groups in Enemy Territory
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is *Sabaki*? (Creating quick, flexible, resilient life in hostile zones).
    *   Key Sabaki techniques: Attachments (*tsuke*), cross-cuts, sacrifices.
    *   Using contact plays to force response and create shape quickly.
    *   "Don't heavy-connect: Sabaki demands flexibility."
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Yang Yilun, *The Fundamental Principles of Go* / *Sabaki: The Art of Handling Weak Groups*.
    *   *Video:* In Sente, *Sabaki Concepts in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Sabaki*.

---

### Week 23: Aji — Potential, Flaws, and Tactical Latency
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is *Aji*? ("Taste" or lingering latent possibilities in settled positions).
    *   Good Aji vs. Bad Aji / *Aji-keshi* (Destroying your own potential too early).
    *   Leaving Aji for later: Why you shouldn't cash in forcing moves prematurely.
    *   Exploiting opponent's bad Aji to launch tactical surprise attacks.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Kageyama Toshiro, *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* (Chapter on Aji).
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Understanding Aji and Aji-Keshi* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Aji*.

---

### Week 24: Asking Moves (*Kikashi*) — Forcing Responses for Strategic Information
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   What is a *Kikashi*? (A light forcing move that requires immediate answer).
    *   "Asking moves": Probing your opponent's intentions before committing your main plan.
    *   How opponent's choice of response determines your global direction.
    *   Avoiding *Aji-keshi* while playing probes.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Takagi Shoichi, *Asking Moves (Kikashi)* / *Probing Moves*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *The Art of Probing Moves in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Kikashi*.

---

### Week 25: Midgame Calculation & Reading — Building Depth (3–5 Moves Ahead)
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   How to read efficiently: Tree-reading vs. disciplined candidate selection.
    *   Reading sequences: "If I play A, they play B, I play C..."
    *   Checking liberty counts in real-time (*Semiai* / Capturing races).
    *   *Eye vs. No Eye* capturing races (*Me-ari Me-nashi*).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Richard Bozulich, *The Second Book of Go* / James Davies, *Reading and Calculation*.
    *   *Video:* Clossius, *How to Read Ahead in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Reading*.

---

### Week 26: Whole-Board Judgement — Positional Analysis & Counting
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   How to estimate score mid-game: Strategic counting.
    *   Counting secured territory vs. potential / points in progress.
    *   Evaluating thickness and weakness in terms of points.
    *   Adapting strategy based on score: Playing solid when ahead, playing risky/aggressive when behind.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Tsukuba, *Positional Judgment* / Cho Chikun, *Positional Analysis*.
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *How to Count Points Mid-Game* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Positional Judgment*.

---

### Week 27: Systematic Endgame (Yose) — Micro-Calculations on 19x19
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Detailed valuation of 1st and 2nd line moves.
    *   Calculating swing values (e.g., 6-point Sente vs. 10-point Gote).
    *   Proper order of execution in late midgame / early endgame.
    *   How games are won or lost in the final 30 moves.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Tomoko Ogawa & James Davies, *The Endgame*.
    *   *Video:* Go Magic, *Mastering the Endgame in Go* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Endgame Values Table*.

---

### Week 28: AI in Modern Go Study — Using KataGo / Leela Zero Responsibly
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   How AI changed Go theory (Early 3-3, shoulder hits, light play).
    *   How to use AI analysis tools (KaTrain, Sabaki + KataGo, OGS AI analysis).
    *   Common pitfalls of studying with AI: Over-memorization vs. understanding principles.
    *   Reviewing your own club games with AI metrics (Winrate graphs vs. Loss-in-points per move).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Software Tool:* [KaTrain (KataGo GUI)](https://github.com/sanderland/katrain).
    *   *Video:* Michael Redmond 9p, *How to Study with AI Tools* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *KataGo / AI Go Theory*.

---

### Week 29: Complex Life & Death & Ko Battles in Real Games
*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Evaluating life and death on the edge and corners under pressure.
    *   Types of Ko: Direct Ko, Two-step Ko, Approach Ko, Eternal Ko.
    *   Ko threats (*Ko-date*): Finding, counting, and prioritizing threat sizes.
    *   When to resolve a Ko vs. when to trade (*Furi-kowake* / Local swap).
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* James Davies, *Life and Death* / *All About Ko*.
    *   *Video:* Nick Sibicky, *Understanding Ko Fights and Threat Counting* (YouTube).
    *   *Article:* Sensei's Library, *Ko Battles*.

*   **Lecture Topics:**
    *   Comprehensive review of the 30-week journey (30k → 10k).
    *   How to self-review games after every match (The 3 most critical mistakes rule).
    *   Etiquette of Go (*Manner/Reigi*): Post-game analysis (*Kento*), respecting opponent, clock management.
    *   Roadmap from 10 Kyu to 1 Dan (SDK to Dan level milestone planning).
    *   Closing Mini-Tournament and graduation certificate presentation.
*   **Recommended References:**
    *   *Book:* Toshiro Kageyama, *Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go* (Final Chapters).
